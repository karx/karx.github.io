# Abstract: What Agents Are Teaching Us

The conversation around AI agents has mostly been about what they can do. This talk asks the harder question: what are they doing to us?

After months of building with AI systems — shipping agentic pipelines, building tools that loop and reason and act — four patterns have surfaced that go beyond technique. They touch something about how we work, how we store knowledge, how we write software, and how we learn to trust systems we can't fully see.

**This talk covers:**

1. **Agency as the unlock** — why the virality of AI agents is really a mirror, reflecting the characteristic we most want in our teams and in ourselves.

2. **Knowledge as infrastructure** — why PKM practitioners and structured-wiki builders were quietly building skill surfaces before skills were a thing, and why agents are the runtime their second brain was waiting for.

3. **Code is free; ecosystem is hard** — what it means to author systems that author software, and why governing code is the new engineering frontier.

4. **Legibility as craft** — why the engineers who will matter most in this era study the pattern of the run, not just the output. The prompt is a seed; the context is what grows.

The talk is presented live using **kaaroViewer** — a spatial intelligence platform that encodes knowledge as a navigable 3D graph. The content of the talk itself is an intelligence brief, explored on canvas in real time. Embraching the HTML.


**Duration:** 30–45 minutes  
**Format:** Talk + Live Demo  
**Audience:** Engineers, AI practitioners, knowledge workers, PKM builders  
**Presented by:** Kartik Arora
